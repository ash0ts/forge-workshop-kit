# Present and share the workshops

Use this packet to introduce the work in a meeting, show one recorded example, or invite someone to try an exercise. The full workshops are technical previews; their external-account and participant rehearsals are still open.

[Open the workshop packet](https://ash0ts.github.io/forge-workshop-kit/packet/) · [Choose a workshop](use-the-workshops.md#choose-the-workflow) · [Setup instructions](setup.md)

## A one-minute introduction

I've been building six hands-on Python workshops for engineers learning to use Forge. Each starts with a small project: handle a refund, forecast solar activity, route a support request, inspect a circuit board, build a departure board, or investigate an airline booking agent.

The exercises teach the work around an agent or model. Participants write code, inspect what it did, test a change, and recover the exact files behind the result. Forge is where they follow the agent's calls, compare experiments and cases, and save the versions they want to reproduce.

Each workshop has an illustrated Report, a deck, an editable notebook, and recorded outcomes. People can read an example, use part of it in a demo, or copy the notebook into their own project. The recorded comparisons include improvements, ties, and regressions.

The next step is to pilot the exercises with people who haven't used the kit. I'd like to find which examples match the work your team is doing and where a new participant gets stuck.

## A ten-minute meeting

| Time | Show | Say or ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 min | The introduction above | Explain who the material is for and what someone can do with it. |
| 1–2 min | The six workshop choices in the packet | Pick one project relevant to the audience. Don't tour all six decks. |
| 2–7 min | One recorded example from the [demo guide](current-demo-guide.md) | Follow one input, a code change, and its output. Identify which part ran and which result was recorded. |
| 7–9 min | The linked Forge record or its labeled capture | Ask what the result establishes and what it leaves uncertain. |
| 9–10 min | The notebook link and [setup guide](setup.md) | Ask someone to try the first exercise and report the step where they needed help. |

For a general engineering audience, start with Pending's refund or timeout. For ML engineers, use a Solar forecast or an Inspection miss. Routing suits a release discussion; Transit suits agent coordination. Airline demonstrates the path from observed behavior to regression cases, using recorded material for its unqualified native monitoring activities.

Use the PDFs if a live page is unavailable. Keep product captures labeled as recorded. A meeting demonstration does not require training, inference, a live ARIA chat, or automatic tag judging. The [demo guide](current-demo-guide.md) gives the exact records and questions.

## Follow-up for a technical pilot

If you'd like to try one, pick the project closest to your work and open its notebook from the packet. Copy it into your own Forge project, initialize the controls, then complete the access check and first exercise. You use your own credentials; the facilitator only reads progress you share.

Please send the workshop name, step, expected result, and what happened. Leave keys and tokens out. The [setup guide](setup.md) explains access, local execution, and recovery. The $8 notebook inference allowance is adjustable; notebook compute, ARIA, and native tag judging are billed separately.

## What is ready to show

Before scheduling a hands-on class, finish the external-account and human checks in [current status](../next-up.md). Airline's AI Alignment and prepared Insights activities remain unqualified. ARIA recordings are available when live access is unavailable. In Airline, closing a notebook does not turn off automatic judging; disable **Apply with AI** when a live tagging activity ends.

Keep claims tied to the [recorded outcomes](recorded-results.md).
